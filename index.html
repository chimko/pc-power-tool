PC Power Supply & FPS Calculation Guide

How does Wattbench calculate recommended PSU wattage?

We sum the real rated power draw of every component you select — CPU PL2/MTP or PPT, GPU TGP/TBP, motherboard, RAM, cooling, and storage — plus a small fixed overhead for fans and peripherals. The recommended PSU size targets roughly 70% load at that peak, which lines up with standard PSU efficiency-curve guidance and matches published real-world PSU recommendations we cross-checked our numbers against.

Why do GPU transient spikes matter?

Modern high-end graphics cards can briefly draw far more than their rated power for a fraction of a second. Under the ATX 3.0/3.1 and PCIe CEM 5.1 specs, PSUs with a native 12VHPWR/12V-2x6 connector are built with the capacitance to absorb these spikes internally. That means the fix is buying a compliant PSU and seating the cable properly — not simply buying a bigger number.

How accurate are the gaming FPS estimates?

Each title is modeled with its own GPU-bound/CPU-bound weighting (for example Valorant leans CPU-bound, Cyberpunk 2077 at Ultra leans GPU-bound) and calibrated against published benchmark reference points. This is a calibrated estimate for ballpark planning, not a live per-title benchmark database — treat exact numbers as directional.

How often is hardware data updated?

Our component database targets a bi-monthly refresh as new desktop parts and PSU revisions launch. CPU power figures for 6th-9th gen Intel parts are derived from a documented methodology (a TDP-based multiplier, explained in the codebase) rather than a single disputed spec number, since independent testing sources disagree on real PL2 values for that era.
